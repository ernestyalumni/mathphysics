# Mathematical physics as an explorable game

## Story

As a mathphysics user, I ALSO want to experience mathematical physics as an interactive video game, where I can explore places, manipulate mathematical objects, try experiments and discover relationships, so that I can develop intuition and perform serious research through interactions that go beyond a conventional IDE or notebook.

I want this experience to evolve through experiments with new interfaces. Start with the readable, inviting exploration and discovery of Stardew Valley, Super Mario World, SNES-era The Legend of Zelda and Super Metroid. Borrow their useful interaction patterns while inventing mechanics suited to mathematics. A later StarCraft/Age of Empires inspired strategy experience may have its own controls, layout and capabilities. Grand Theft Auto is another broad reference for open-world freedom, rather than the first design target.

Explore Physics MUST launch separately as a standalone game experience, designed from the ground up around exploration and direct interaction. Its map, contextual tools and field journal have their own layout, controls and visual identity. Notebook remains a separate experience with its own Notebook/Pixel styles; those styles do not govern Explore.

## Scene-first correction

Explore MUST “unshackle” itself from any notion of a notebook, beyond removing its shell. Interactivity MUST be the most prominent experience. A standalone page with a long SU(2) worksheet of controls, matrix and four plots does not satisfy this requirement. A static page stack of controls and plots MUST NOT be the primary interface.

The default lab MUST present a dominant interactive viewport, a directly manipulable mathematical object such as an SU(2) rotor, and a compact contextual HUD. Map/world locations and spatial tools MUST support immediately usable mathematical interactions. Detailed control/mathematics sheets and the field journal MUST be hidden until requested; dismissing them MUST restore the same scene, parameters, playback state and unsaved work. Contextual readouts MUST be legible and truthful. Precise equations, axes, conventions, assumptions, source references and evidence MUST remain inspectable on request. Numerical checks, exact computation and formal proof MUST remain distinct; animation or a discovery badge proves none of them.

Stardew Valley, SNES Super Mario World, The Legend of Zelda and Super Metroid guide navigation, discovery, tools, atmosphere and interactivity. Super Metroid does not require combat or difficult platforming. No commercial sprites, maps, fonts or sounds may be copied without permission. Available mathematical tools and evidence MUST NOT require traversal, unlocks or game progression. Direct access, keyboard alternatives, touch alternatives to hover/precision dragging, visible focus, reduced motion and user-controlled sound MUST remain available.

See [scene-first acceptance and parent handoff](../docs/plans/rust-physics-platform/game-exploration/SCENE-FIRST.md).

## Requirements

- Explore MUST have an independent direct entry and launcher, with no dependency on Notebook shell JavaScript, CSS or document model. Opening or refreshing its direct URL MUST work without first visiting Notebook. A user MUST be able to complete the exploration workflow without ever opening Notebook.
- Explore MUST NOT use notebook tabs, input/output cells, source-editor metaphors, Mathematica vocabulary or a forced notebook workflow. It MUST NOT display “pin to notebook” controls or require a Notebook button to enter or operate it. Precise equations, numerical values, assumptions, source references and accessible evidence remain essential.
- Shared Rust backends and domain representations are allowed. Future explicit data exchange with Notebook is optional, not an entry, save or evidence prerequisite. Separation MUST preserve existing experiments, field-journal entries, notes, saved notebooks and unsaved work; unsupported data MUST remain recoverable with an explicit explanation, never silently deleted.
- The first exploration MUST support direct manipulation, immediate feedback and a short discoverable route from entering a place to performing a mathematical experiment. It MUST also offer direct access for a researcher who already knows the desired object. Spatial traversal and game progression MUST NOT be prerequisites for accessing available mathematical tools or evidence.
- Use a map, experiment locations, contextual tools and a field journal as the initial game interaction model. These are starting design proposals, not a requirement to clone commercial games or retain every familiar game mechanic. Optional challenges MUST coexist with free experimentation. Exploration achievements MUST NOT imply that a theorem has been proved.
- The first mathematical destination SHOULD be an SU(2) laboratory centered on direct scene manipulation, with linked complex coordinates, 3D views, cross-sections and parameter/time tools available contextually or on request. Each interaction MUST change the underlying mathematical state and all linked views consistently, rather than playing a decorative animation.
- Users MUST be able to play, pause, scrub, step and reset an explicitly defined parameter t. Label whether t denotes a dimensionless path parameter, an angle, or physical time; physical-time evolution MUST declare its generator and units. Reset and replay MUST be reproducible.
- Every plot MUST make its mathematical map, axes, selected values, conventions and limitations inspectable on request, with essential chart/slice identity and invalid/off-chart status visible contextually. A slice, projection, orbit and the full underlying space MUST be distinguishable. Singularities and off-chart points MUST be visible as such, never silently clipped into a false finite value.
- Users MUST be able to inspect equations and numerical readouts, record an experiment with its assumptions and settings, compare runs and reopen it. Field-journal entries MUST retain source/PDF references and calculation evidence independently of Notebook. Links or exchange with notebook cells may be considered later as an optional capability. The same experiment representation SHOULD eventually be usable by available AI agents and harnesses; only working adapters may appear connected.
- Rust remains the primary mathematical engine and native service language; TypeScript owns browser interaction/rendering. Reuse existing Rust Lie-group capabilities where verified. Any GPU renderer MUST distinguish visual sampling from validated computation. Browser WebGL/WebGPU and native CUDA/C++ require measured capability and compatibility decisions; CUDA is not assumed available in browsers or on macOS.
- Readable mathematics, keyboard alternatives, visible focus, reduced motion and user-controlled sound MUST survive the game presentation. Color, audio, precise movement and reaction speed MUST NOT be the only ways to inspect or operate an experiment. Provide pause and direct controls.
- Evaluate existing interactive math demos and preserve references and applicable licenses before adopting code or assets. Game inspiration does not authorize copying commercial sprites, maps, fonts or sounds.
- The design MUST remain iterative. Evaluate a small standalone playable slice on actual mathematical tasks, collect confusion points, revise controls and record findings. Any later comparison with Notebook is a separate usability study, not part of the Explore workflow. Do not equate game-like decoration with successful exploration.

## First proposed playable loop: SU(2) observatory

Enter an observatory from a small explorable map, or open it directly through a command/search action. Select an axis and a path through SU(2). Manipulate the scene rotor immediately to move along that path, with contextual readouts. Request a tool or mathematics sheet to inspect synchronized complex coordinates, a labeled 3D chart, slice or matrix, rather than a default four-plot page stack. Pause at a point, compare a previous point, and open the field journal on request to save the experiment. Closing a sheet preserves the scene and mathematical state. Optional discovery: investigate why a full physical turn can change a spinor's sign while a second turn restores it. Show the mathematics behind the event.

A prior browser level was implemented inside the shared workbench; the historical evidence below does not establish delivery of this standalone correction. Top-down exploration is a first prototype candidate; side-scrolling traversal and a strategy command room are separate later experiments.

## Independent acceptance scenarios

1. **Independent start and interaction:** From a fresh browser session, a user launches Explore directly, refreshes its URL successfully, opens the SU(2) lab by map or direct contextual action, manipulates an object, inspects equations/evidence, saves and reopens a field-journal entry, and never visits Notebook. Verify that this entry loads no Notebook shell JavaScript, CSS or document-model dependency. No notebook tabs, cells, editor workflow, Mathematica vocabulary or “pin to notebook” controls appear.
2. **Linked SU(2) state:** Changing an axis, matrix parameter, time dial or plot selection updates every displayed representation from one versioned state. The displayed matrix remains unitary with determinant one within an explicitly declared numerical tolerance. A malformed/non-finite input is rejected visibly. No UI-only normalization disguises an invalid result.
3. **Correct geometry:** The SU(2) object is identified with S³; any 3D image is explicitly a chart/projection/slice. A Bloch sphere, if included, is labeled S² and represents the appropriate state/orbit construction, not all of SU(2). Projection poles have a deliberate representation.
4. **Reproducible path:** With the proposed convention U(t)=exp(-i t n·σ/2), unit n and dimensionless angular t, independent checks give U(0)=I, U(2π)=-I and U(4π)=I within tolerance. Scrubbing to the same t gives the same state regardless of frame rate or prior play speed. A sign change is not erased by quaternion sign canonicalization.
5. **Meaningful slices:** Moving a slice control displays the equation, fixed coordinate and valid range. The view distinguishes an empty slice, a degenerate boundary and an ordinary intersection. Turning animation off leaves the same inspectable state.
6. **Evidence and save:** Saving, reloading and replaying an experiment preserves convention version, parameters, chart/slice selection, t, source links and calculation status. A journal discovery is labeled observed/numerically checked/exactly computed as appropriate; only real proof evidence can produce a proof claim. Unsupported schema versions or failed calculations do not fabricate progress.
7. **Accessible operation:** A keyboard-only user can enter the lab, change parameters, pause/step/reset, read values and save a result without moving an avatar precisely. Reduced motion suppresses optional movement; muting retains all outcome feedback.
8. **Preserved data:** Existing experiment and notes data remain recoverable after separation; reopening Explore preserves saved experiment state and evidence without loading Notebook. Existing Notebook documents and drafts remain intact in that separate experience. Optional future exchange does not gate any Explore action.
9. **Iterative usability:** Observe representative users completing “open the SU(2) lab,” “compare two points” and “save and reopen an experiment.” Record task completion, wrong turns and interpretation errors, including whether the projection was mistaken for the full space. Revise before expanding the world.

10. **Scene-first default:** At desktop and touch viewport sizes, direct launch and lab entry show a dominant interactive scene with an immediately usable rotor and contextual HUD. No long worksheet, static control/matrix/four-plot stack or open journal is the primary view. Open controls/math/journal on request, then dismiss them without losing scene, parameters, playback state or unsaved work.
11. **Accessible world tools:** Map selection and direct access reach the same available experiment/tools without traversal or unlocks. Keyboard-only and touch users can manipulate, pause/step/reset, inspect equations/evidence, save and reopen without hover-only actions, precise avatar movement or precision dragging. Contextual readouts reflect actual linked mathematical state; numerical, exact and proof claims remain distinct.
