### Red-team check of the proposed recurrence

Here is a four-direction extension of N2's rectangle identity (its lines 80–90). This is a reviewer derivation for separate checking. It uses precisely the positive transfer spectra and rectangular trace formulas in G02/C23. It may substantially reduce the scope of the open recurrence in lattice.tex:771–777.

Fix β and n≥1. Write F(x)=log Z for a rectangle with four side lengths x. Let Dᵢ double side i, and put pᵢ(x)=2F(x)−F(Dᵢx). All pᵢ are nonnegative. There are two elementary estimates.

1. At fixed transverse sides, if p=pᵢ(x)≤1/4, then pᵢ(Dᵢx)≤4p². Indeed the top-normalized tail s at the original time length obeys s≤exp(p)−1≤p exp(p). At doubled length the tail is at most s². The log impurity there is at most twice that tail, giving 2 exp(2p)p²≤4p². This includes p=0.
2. For i≠j, commutation of the two doublings gives the exact identity
   pᵢ(Dⱼx)=2pᵢ(x)−2pⱼ(x)+pⱼ(Dᵢx). Expanding all four terms verifies the signs.

Let δ=Δ(n). On the first cube of side choices {n,2n}, every edge cost pᵢ with xᵢ=n is ≤δ. Choose any doubling path from the all-n rectangle to the all-2n rectangle through that edge. Its weighted sum of nonnegative costs is δ, and every weight is at least one.

Now keep xᵢ=n and allow exactly j transverse sides of length 4n, the others being n or 2n. Claim pᵢ(x)≤Aⱼ=3^j δ for j=0,1,2,3, provided 27δ≤1/4. The previous paragraph starts the induction. For j>0 halve a 4n side h to get y with yₕ=2n. The identity gives pᵢ(x)≤2pᵢ(y)+pₕ(Dᵢy). The first cost is ≤Aⱼ₋₁. For the second, halve side h of Dᵢy once more to length n. There are j−1 transverse sides of length 4n and side i now has length 2n. The induction hypothesis in direction h bounds this shorter cost by Aⱼ₋₁. Estimate 1 then bounds the second cost by 4Aⱼ₋₁². Since Aⱼ₋₁≤1/4, the total is ≤3Aⱼ₋₁.

On the path from the all-2n rectangle to the all-4n rectangle, the step with j previously doubled sides has cost at most 4Aⱼ² by estimate 1 applied to its length-n predecessor. Hence

$$
\Delta(2n)\le 4(8+4\cdot9+2\cdot81+729)\,\delta^2
=3740\,\delta^2 .
$$

Taking c₁=3740 and δ≤1/7480 meets every smallness condition, including 27δ≤1/4. The constant is deliberately loose. The proof uses no dynamical input capable of locating a starting box. A uniform bound on its physical size along the cutoff sequence and the continuum steps of lattice.tex:821ff remain separate obligations.

**Verification limit.** I compared the Lean declarations and proof inputs at the pinned commit. I did not rebuild Lean, formalize this rectangle induction, audit the full N2 paper, or establish the bounded-scale hypothesis.

## 2. C23 transfer positivity — accept with corrections

The sketch is in `spectral-theory-study-route.md:26–36` and ledger C23/C23a. The TeX presents the kernel and trace and retains positivity as a hypothesis (lattice.tex:638–671,718–721).

- **Gauge projection and character signs, sketch:28–32.** Inverting the transformed link gives exactly the adjacent temporal links in the kernel. Product Haar invariance gives P*=P=P². Simultaneous gauge transformation conjugates each single-link difference at its starting vertex. Thus K₀ commutes with P. Normalized character orthogonality diagonalizes the single-link convolution with scalar aλ/dλ. The exponential series converges uniformly because |tr g|≤m. Its character coefficients are sums of nonnegative tensor multiplicities for β≥0, times the positive Wilson constant. Smit, printed pp. 102–105, Eqs. (4.135), (4.138), (4.150)–(4.161), gives the projected transfer, scalar and singlet-count argument.
- **Trace and gap, sketch:33–34, lattice.tex:649–670.** Positivity survives tensor product, compression and multiplication on both sides. Compactness, continuity, symmetry and the full-support Haar measure now give the Mercer diagonal trace formula, including n=1. Gluing n kernels counts each plaquette once with its Wilson weight. The manuscript inference at line 670 is premature. Symmetry and continuity alone give a self-adjoint Hilbert–Schmidt operator. Move the trace-class inference after positivity or supply a separate nuclear factorization. A strictly positive continuous kernel improves positivity, giving a simple top eigenvalue by Jentzsch. Compactness separates it from the rest of the spectrum at fixed volume. State the rank-one convention from G02 at sketch:34 and `ledger/claims.md:30`. The gauge projection itself can have a nullspace.
- **Numerical evidence.** Re-ran `ledger/evidence/transfer-positivity/check_su2_character_coefficients.py:4–23`. All 21 cases passed. Multiplying the two sines in the normalized Haar integral gives I₂ⱼ−I₂ⱼ₊₂=2(2j+1)I₂ⱼ₊₁/β, for the weight with exp(−β) factored out. The general β and group statement follows from the preceding character argument.

**Verification limit.** I checked the finite-volume argument in every coordinate direction and Smit's relevant transfer and character equations. I did not reconstruct Lüscher's 1977 proof or verify claims about which references other books omit.

## 3. W04, Faizal–Shabir v1 — accept with corrections

I checked the four numbered objections in `YM-W04-faizal-shabir.md:15–20` against the local v1 TeX. Both hashes at lines 7–8, the 593-page count and the 21,093-line count agree.

1. **Fixed strong-coupling input, W04:15.** Source `main.tex:2247–2251,2263–2269,4411,4454–4463,13894–13929` supports the objection. Blocking b fine steps at a fixed starting cutoff moves the coarse spacing upward. A construction with a *family* of increasingly fine starts could use blocking, but needs estimates uniform in the starting cutoff and matched couplings. The authors do mention tuning at 3855 and βₖ at 15743ff. The gap estimate given here does not supply that bridge. Minor transcription at W04:15: the source at 2249 prints V on both sides, although its stated map type at 2251 requires the left adjoint as written in W04.
2. **Scale direction and uniform constants, W04:16.** Confirmed at 2267,2277 versus 2671,3532,4551. Companion III explicitly recognizes the reversal at 15732 and reuses the inequality with refined indexing. Reindexing a one-sided estimate does not reverse its direction. At 2277, nonnegativity of Δₖ also fails to imply Δₖ≤1, which the exponential majorization requires. On a refining sequence the coefficient at 4435 is at least 1/aₖ₊₁. On a coarsening sequence the factor aₖ/aₖ₊₁ in 4432 is 1/b and cannot be discarded in the lower bound at 4433.
3. **One-loop sign, W04:17.** Confirmed for the direction actually called coarse-graining at 3855–3862. With μ dg/dμ=−b₀g³ and μ' = μ/b, one gets g'=g+b₀ log(b)g³+O(g⁵). A decreasing g has the UV sign when following a refining sequence. Using that convention here would require reconciling it with the direction of the transfer estimate.
4. **Spectral theorem, W04:18.** Its conditional form is accurately reported. Being conditional is not itself an error. There is a direct error *inside* the proposed implication. The lower operator bound at 4398–4403 and 4413–4417 is used as an upper bound on the excited subspace at 4421–4424. For Π=I, R=0, T=diag(1,1/2), T'=diag(1,3/4), the hypothesis holds while the asserted upper bound fails on the second vector. Moreover 4436–4438 needs the total weighted loss strictly smaller than the starting gap. Summability alone permits a larger loss. This invalidates the displayed gap transport even before passage to a continuum limit.

**Editorial corrections and limit.** W04:20,34,59 should confine the verdict to the cited argument in v1. W04:46 is accurate about the absence of “OpenAI” but its Bałaban claim needs a main-paper qualifier. Companion discussions at 11932,14932,15467 compare or credit multiscale methods. I did not certify every lemma or alternative proof in the companions, the horizon projection's effect on the measure, the version history, or the precise historical priority attributed to Osterwalder–Seiler at W04:15,29,53. The concrete contradictions above suffice for the local review of this argument.

## 4. N03 thermodynamic integration — accept with corrections

I independently integrated the archived plaquette means in Python, using equal replica weights, composite Simpson weights and the stated independent-error prescription. This reproduces the displayed 2D SU(2), 4D SU(2) n=2, and U(1) n=2 estimates to rounding. It also reproduces the n=4 **increments from β=1** using the twelve small-box and fifteen large-box replicas specified in `YM-N03-defect-measurement.md:65–69`. The tabulations are informative even where their interpretation fails.

- **Lines 18–26.** For the manuscript weight exp[−βΣ(1−a₀)], ∂β log Z=Nₚ(⟨a₀⟩−1). The printed single-volume integral and 2D character sum use the shifted weight exp(βΣa₀). The extensive shift cancels in Δ and log Pₖ. The integrand for log Pₖ uses large minus small, the reverse of the defect sign. `Rust/lattice-gauge/examples/defect_ti.rs:20–30,51–57` implements that cancellation and sign correctly.
- **Lines 24,40,52,66–69.** Jackknife errors quantify sampling under the blocking and independence assumptions. Each CPU β scan anneals the same chain (`plaquette_scan.rs:20–33`), so independence across grid points is an approximation. Adjacent log purities share a partition-function estimate. Use the covariance of the actual linear combination for a statistical test. The telescope constructed from common log Z values is an algebraic identity even on noisy data. The checks of individual signs are meaningful consistency checks. Simpson discretization error is absent from the quoted bars. For example at n=4, β=2.2, Simpson integration on the saved h=0.1 grid gives 9.318(79), while using every other point gives 10.245. The difference illustrates resolution sensitivity, not a calibrated error bound.
- **Lines 26–36.** The five validation rows agree with the character formula to their quoted precision. Solving that same formula at n=2 gives β=2.45922 for Δ=1/4 and **3.87706** for Δ=log 2. The quoted 3.6 is wrong. The measured Δ at 3.6 is 0.60789(748).
- **Lines 42–61.** The printed n=2 mass entries are plug-in estimates of the right-hand side and their arithmetic checks. The factor of increase from β=1 to 2 is 43.61/1.456≈30, rather than two orders of magnitude. Only one direction is chosen as Euclidean time at once. A symmetric finite torus has no thermodynamic phase transition. A bulk thermal critical coupling is defined with growing spatial volume at fixed temporal extent. Peak positions or small defects on symmetric boxes cannot establish necessity or sufficiency for confinement.
- **Lines 59–61,69–87.** A near-zero derivative *above* β₀ cannot determine the missing integral *below* β₀. The estimator itself labels its output Δ−Δ(β₀) (`defect_window.rs:25–40`). The n=4 results at 1.8 and 2.0 are −0.0032(506) and 0.3362(632) plus the unknown Δ₁(4). Positivity supplies a lower bound on the missing constant, whereas claiming smallness needs an upper bound. The negative estimate cannot be put in the logarithm. Even taking the offset as zero at 2.0 gives a central mass expression **0.2293**, not ≥0.24. A finite-confidence lower bound needs an upper confidence limit for the *absolute* defect, including systematic errors. The quoted 1.97 crossing is conditional on this normalization and interpolation. The predicted smallness through β≈2.2–2.3 fails already at 2.2, where the increment alone is about 9.32.
- **Lines 14,59–61,81–87,103–107.** The number 1/4 is the largest possible threshold for c₁≥2. Passing that cut does not establish the seed at the actual c₁. The length arithmetic with Teper's data gives roughly 4 and 8 lattice spacings and a nominal 1.24 fm at n=4, β=2.0 under the quoted scale convention. The central defect there already exceeds 1/4. The physical-size *hypothesis* does not follow. At β=1.8 the measured Δ(2)=25.68 fails the antecedent of the recursion by a wide margin. A single decrease with n, including in the proposed U(1) comparison, neither tests a quadratic law in its small-defect regime nor diagnoses a continuum gap. The U(1) derivative actually falls from about 249 to 18 over the displayed interval. A nonzero value at the last point cannot identify its cause or its asymptotics. [Teper, Table 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/9812187)

**Interpretation.** For the logical implication in G02 the recursion propagates a seed. For an application to Yang–Mills, obtaining that seed with a uniform physical-size bound is independent dynamical content. The finite-volume derivation in §1 makes the distinction particularly sharp. Neither the n=2 data nor the n=4 increments justify N03:61's identification of the recursion as the remaining physical input.

**Verification limit.** I used saved scan summaries and checked the integration and inferred numerical scales. I did not regenerate gauge chains, validate seeds or update counts, certify equilibration or autocorrelation errors, or bound the n=4 offset.

## 5. T19 topology and vortex measurements — accept with corrections

The saved vortex dumps reproduce the reported finite-field count differences. The bundle classification, the proposed θ filter, and the interpretation of that count as a single crosscap require substantive corrections. Line numbers below refer to `YM-T19-algebraic-topology-and-the-gap.md`.

- **Lines 7–8,28,56,64.** A six-component class w classifies the obstruction to lifting a PSU(N) bundle to SU(N) on T⁴. Fixing w still leaves an integer instanton label. For example, w=0 allows different second Chern numbers. The periodic sector fixes the centre twist. With an oriented basis and the note's charge sign, take an integral lift u of w on T⁴. Exterior multiplication gives ∫u²=2(n₀₁n₂₃−n₀₂n₁₃+n₀₃n₁₂). The fractional charge is −Pf(n)/N mod 1 in that convention. Changing the lift by Nv changes (∫u²)/(2N) by an integer since the intersection form is even. This argument on T⁴ also specifies the lift convention for odd N. The relation of the square to the *gauge* characteristic number is extra bundle theory. A test of the cup product cannot derive that normalization. The actual cochain routine in `Rust/algebraic-topology/src/simplicial.rs:97–103` takes an integer lift of a **mod-2 cocycle** and computes the square modulo 4 when reduced. Its tests support the stated representative changes in that domain. They do not implement general Z_N backgrounds. [’t Hooft, twisted self-dual solutions](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01208900)
- **Lines 9–10,20–24,64.** It is the sector partition functions (Boltzmann weights) that undergo a discrete Fourier projection over temporal twists at fixed spatial magnetic twist. Taking logs afterward defines the flux free energies. Spatial twists need separate interpretation. Confining electric flux can cost string energy proportional to length. Identifying the approach of a twisted ratio to one with the lightest neutral glueball correlation length needs an additional argument relating distinct sectors and observables. The estimator alone supplies none. For periodic projected links z the plaquette cochain is δz, hence its full dual sheet has zero ambient mod-2 homology class even when individual components wind. Intrinsic homology is a different measurement. The dual magnetic currents are closed by lattice current conservation. Göpfert–Mack concerns 3D compact U(1) with the Villain action. It does not identify a gauge-projected SU(2) percolation criterion. [von Smekal and de Forcrand](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/0209149), [Göpfert and Mack](https://bib-pubdb1.desy.de/record/397063)
- **Lines 12–16,65.** In pure SU(N), an area law for lines charged under the electric centre one-form symmetry signals its unbroken realization. For SU(2) at θ=π there is a mixed time reversal/centre anomaly. In a conventional orientation its background phase is exp(±iπ∫P(B)/2), with P(B) evaluated mod 4. Anomaly matching excludes a unique, symmetry-preserving, trivially gapped vacuum there. For example two time-reversed vacua can each have a positive excitation gap. A proof bounding the gap at both angles can therefore be consistent. The filter applies to a claim that also fixes the symmetry realization and rules out degeneracy and topological order. [Generalized Global Symmetries](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.5148), [Theta, Time Reversal, and Temperature, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.00501)
- **Lines 18,52–54.** Lüscher's small-plaquette condition is sufficient for a geometric lattice charge. A necessity theorem for all continuum constructions is a different claim. After cooling, the code actually compares counts to round(1.15 Q_clover) and uses a persistence threshold or basin-charge cut (`examples/instanton_persistence.rs:51–76` in the topology crate). At the default threshold 0.002 I reproduced 12/12 peak agreements on 8⁴ and 8/30 on 12⁴, with 12/30 basin agreements on 12⁴. This tests a counting procedure. Failure may reflect segmentation, cooling or overlap. It cannot decide what generates the mass gap. [Lüscher, Topology of lattice gauge fields](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02029132)
- **Lines 31–35,46–48,66.** For a finite 2-complex, H₂ over ℤ is free. The universal coefficient theorem gives b₂(Fₚ)=rank H₂(ℤ)+tₚ(H₁), where tₚ counts cyclic primary factors whose orders are powers of p. However `examples/vortex_surfaces.rs:43–50` computes the whole sheet over F₂ **and F₁₀₀₀₀₀₃**. The reported difference is exactly t₂−t₁₀₀₀₀₃. A difference of one proves at least one even-order torsion factor. Equality to exactly one requires excluding torsion at the comparison prime. Even a true rational rank would not distinguish ℤ/2 from ℤ/4 or ℤ/8. Branching invalidates an inference to a surface crosscap. An integral Smith form or certified ranks and invariant factors, component by component, would settle the algebraic claim. The measured largest area fraction also does not test spanning or nontrivial winding by itself.
- **Lines 30–48.** Replaying `vortex_surfaces` with homology enabled gives differences of one in 20/20, 20/20, 10/10, 7/20, 4/20, 1/19 nonempty 8⁴ dumps in increasing β order. On 12⁴ the counts are 20/20 and 14/20. Closure held throughout. The ten reference sheets had 9–17 components and largest shares 0.894–0.962. At β=2.8 the scan summary says 20 configurations. The dump contains IDs 0–18. The writer emits only negative plaquettes (`Rust/lattice-gauge/examples/vortex_scan.rs:45–52`), so an empty last configuration would yield 1/20 overall. Identify the missing ID as empty or unavailable before assigning the ensemble denominator. The 18/20 random-start count at β=2.4 reproduces, but the table documents only 10 default configurations there. Establish pairing before claiming differences configuration by configuration. Four random controls, two symmetric box sizes and a gauge-fixing prescription support an association. They do not isolate confinement, define an infinite-volume order parameter, or establish a per-component or per-configuration lemma.
- **Lines 50,58–60,68–73.** A largest-cluster fraction describes concentration of current; a winding or finite-size test is needed to call it percolation. For the projected Creutz ratios the saved summary giving 0.1357(28) uses **200** configurations at β=2.3. The 20-configuration homology scan at the same parameters reports 0.1348(58). “Same configurations” needs a provenance link or the corrected ensemble size. Teper Table 10 gives a√σ=0.3687(22), hence a²σ≈0.1359, and 0.2686(21) at 2.4. The saved 0.0584(32) at 2.4 gives about 81% within errors. A finite-loop ratio does not determine the asymptotic tension. The quoted 0.1154(8) susceptibility uses rescaled, integer-rounded cooled charge; the same summary gives 0.1075(7) raw. The comparison 0.1163(13) is one method in Table 15. Lines 68–73 describe proposed or running work. [Teper, Tables 10 and 15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/9812187)
