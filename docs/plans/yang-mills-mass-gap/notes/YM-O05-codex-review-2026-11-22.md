## 1. G02b: the finite-box instance

**Seed — accept, with a geometry correction (G:17–29).** For β≥0, normalized product Haar, and the *shifted* Wilson weight, the integrated weight is between `exp(−βP)` and `exp(βP)` because the normalized real plaquette trace has absolute value ≤1. The extensive shift from the unshifted action cancels in Δ. With one positively oriented link per site and axis and one plaquette per site and unordered axis pair, `P(n)=6n⁴`, `P(2n)=16P(n)`. Hence `16 log Z(n)−log Z(2n) ≤ 32βP(n)=192βn⁴`. The denominators 1,436,160 at n=1 and 22,978,560 at n=2 are correct. Retain indexed self-links and repeated incidences on degenerate boxes; do not implement them as a simple graph. On the **all-one** torus a plaquette is a commutator. On a mixed rectangle with just side μ of length one it is `Uμ(x) Uν(x) Uμ(x+ν)⁻¹ Uν(x)⁻¹`; the two μ links need not coincide. The bound still holds, including the comparison of side 1 to side 2. Boundedness proves this seed, not transfer positivity for arbitrary bounded actions.

**(H) — accept for the stated Wilson groups (G:33,41–52).** On a slice let `P=∫R(g) dg` over one group element per spatial site. Normalized Haar gives `P*=P=P²`; don't discard the temporal holonomy or divide by a gauge volume. Equivalently leave every temporal link integrated: the one-step kernel is the gauge average of `V(U)^{1/2} K(U,U′) V(U′)^{1/2}` on the primed slice. The spatial plaquettes are gauge invariant, and simultaneous endpoint transformations conjugate `U_e U′_e⁻¹` at the source vertex, hence the class kernel commutes with the gauge action. The average is `A=P T̃ P`; the product around t periodic slices counts each spatial weight once. Its cyclic integral is `Tr A^t = Tr_{Pℋ} T^t` for t≥1, including the t=1 diagonal with its gauge average. This also proves the residual-projection formula in temporal gauge. In time-only doubling the spatial operator must stay fixed.

For a finite list of links, `Re tr(U_j U_k†)/N` is the Gram matrix of vectorized matrices with the **real**, normalized Frobenius inner product. SU(2) unit quaternions, the U(1) circle and Z₂ signs are special cases. Entrywise powers are PSD by Schur products (power zero is the all-ones kernel); the exponential series has nonnegative coefficients and converges to a PSD kernel for β≥0. A product over links is PSD as well. This is operator positivity, not just pointwise positivity. Continuous PSD kernels on these compact metrizable spaces with full-support Haar satisfy Mercer's trace formula: one-link trace `∫k(u,u)du=e^β`, not necessarily the trace of the **projected** operator. Finite tensoring, bounded multiplication by `√V` on both sides and compression preserve trace class. The continuous gauge-averaged kernel has a strictly positive diagonal integral, so `T≠0`. Construct trace class first and then use the cyclic trace identity; neither needs an assumption that the physical gap is positive. Applies after choosing any axis as time. No Mercer or Haar development was checked in Lean.

**Map to F01 — accept (G:7–13,35–39).** `Rectangle.lean:402–409` also takes `hn : 1 ≤ n` and `hr : ∀ k, 0 ≤ r k`; define `F` arbitrarily on zero-side arguments, which the hypotheses never query. Order eigenvalues *with multiplicities*, normalize by the positive top eigenvalue and pad a missing second eigenvalue by zero. For `s=Σ_{j>1}(λ_j/λ₁)^t`, `q=s_{2t}≤s` and `q≤s²`. Thus `exp(p)=(1+s)²/(1+q)≥1+s`; `p≥0` and `p′≤2 log(1+q)≤2s²≤2e^{2p}p²≤4p²` for `p≤1/4`. For `N=2^k n`, choose time as the **first** doubled direction. Its original spatial side is N, not 2N (last direction in the older telescope). The weights are 8,4,2,1; `8p≤Δ(N)`, so `r_k^N≤s_N≤exp(p)−1≤exp(Δ(N))−1`. This discharges `from_defect`; it really does include k=0 on spatial side n. From `r_k^n≤1/2` take logs only if `r_k>0`; rank one gives ratio zero, extended gap +∞. Distinguish this mathematical instantiation from the compiled abstract implication; I read the declarations, did not run a new Lean build.

**Z₂ — accept as a finite-box target, not a finished formalization (G:54–59).** Even Schur/Mercer are unnecessary here: the normalized one-link matrix has eigenvalues `cosh β` and `sinh β` in the constant/sign basis. Tensor, conjugate by `√V`, average over site signs, prove equality of the periodic finite sum and the trace on the invariant subspace, and apply the spectral inequalities. Include the normalized counting factors, n=1 self-links, and β=0. Each fixed slice is finite-dimensional; the theorem quantifies over their sizes, it is not a single finite test. The infinite-volume limit is not finite-dimensional. SU(2) also needs Haar changes of variables, measurable insertions, spectral summability and trace identities, not just a swap of matrix libraries.

## 2. G02b §3: which limit and which Hilbert space?

**Accept the dyadic local-limit argument with the following specifications; do not cite site reflection or (H) alone as the entire proof (G:63–88).**

1. **Cylinder limit (a).** Fix N first. With `S=T/λ₁`, normalized top vector `h`, and `Q=|h⟩⟨h|`, `‖S^j−Q‖₁=Σ_{l>1}(λ_l/λ₁)^j ≤ r^{j−1} Σ_{l>1} λ_l/λ₁ → 0` for j≥1 (rank one is immediate). `Tr S^j→1`. For an observable in a fixed s-step slab, integrate the slab with its insertion to obtain `B_F` in the same cyclic integral. Before normalization its kernel is dominated in absolute value by `‖F‖∞` times the positive s-step kernel (time-zero slice functions are bounded multipliers). This bounds the insertion operator. The long complementary power converges in trace norm, so the normalized expectation has a limit. The spectral assumptions control the long power; the **insertion identity** uses the local Wilson kernel, not just partition-function traces.
2. **OS vectors at fixed N.** For gauge-invariant bounded continuous local slab functions use half-cylinder integration conditioned on the time-zero spatial links. For example its future boundary vector is obtained by applying the normalized inserted slab to `h`; for `F=1` the vector is `h`. Sewing to the reflected past gives the OS inner product, and inserting t empty layers gives `S^t`. Thus the centered inner product is bounded by `r^t‖F‖∞‖G‖∞` by Cauchy–Schwarz; this includes overlapping *boundaries* at t=0. Use site reflection with the correct orientation/inversion of reflected links and no links crossing outside the positive half. Gauge-invariant insertions keep these vectors in `Pℋ`.

   **Domain correction:** G:65 says *every* bounded F with the transfer matrix on `Pℋ`. At t=0 a gauge-variant spatial-link function can have nonzero OS norm `ω(|F|²)` but zero gauge average. Do not project the shared boundary twice. Either work in the cyclic physical observable sector throughout, or for the full link algebra use `A=P T̃ P` on the **full** slice Hilbert space (extra zero spectrum), sewing without a projection at t=0. Conditional boundary vectors then need not be invariant, but at t≥1 a gauge-averaged empty layer kills their noninvariant part. In particular distinguish uniqueness of the vacuum *in the chosen reconstruction* from uniqueness of Gibbs states.
3. **Spatial limit (b).** Take the time-cylinder limit first, then periodically lift the spatial configurations to the infinite lattice. Compact metrizable group on countably many edges gives subsequential weak compactness. For each fixed support and integer t the supports of the translated/reflected products fit for all sufficiently large N. Weak convergence passes expectations and their products for **continuous local** functions, not for arbitrary bounded measurable cylinder events. Translation invariance, site RP, and `|connected| ≤ q^t‖F‖∞‖G‖∞`, `q=2^(−1/n)`, survive. No limit interchange or uniqueness is needed. These are limit points of the specified cylinders, not a classification of all boundary conditions or an identification with a continuum state.
4. **Density and spectral support (c).** Use the union of continuous local positive-time functions with *all finite slab widths*, not functions of one fixed layer. Their OS classes span a dense subspace of the reconstruction by definition (or by local continuous approximation in L² with OS norm bounded by L²). Centering by `ω(F)1` gives a dense set in `Ω⊥`. One-step positivity does **not** follow merely from translation invariance and site RP. Pass the finite-cylinder form `ω_N(θF̄·τ₁F)≥0` and the contraction bounds on the same dense algebra to the limit, or supply link reflection positivity. Then `0≤S_ω≤1`. For a centered vector and any `b>q`, `b^t μ_F([b,1])≤⟨ψ_F,S_ω^t ψ_F⟩≤C_F q^t`; hence that projection annihilates a dense set, and by continuity all of `Ω⊥`. The fixed vectors are precisely the vacuum line. This proves the stated lattice decay conclusion for those limit states after the interface repairs. It uses neither Dobrushin uniqueness nor the quoted strong-coupling asymptotic; I did not check those literature estimates.

## 3. G02b §5: three audit questions, with source answers

* **Tuning:** The public object is expressly two-indexed: `OpenAI-YM-20261028` at `3456e5e`, `PublicWilson.lean:35–57` (`beta : ℕ → ℕ → ℝ`, moment uses `C.beta N p`); `WilsonBridge.lean:20–26` carries it through; `ChallengeSpec.lean:14` gives a **uniform slope**, not equality in p. This agrees with `01-WHAT-OPENAI-PROVED.md:14,55`. In the informalization `daf1db4`, `manuscript/part1/05-renormalization.tex:240–254` fixes the *coarse* endpoint independently of p; `part2/E-section.tex:198–216` gives unique shooting **for each** N,p. Neither theorem identifies the two bare tunings. That is not a proof that particular values differ either. Also `periodStrict` alone does not provide a period twice every other period. Specify the scaled boxes and bare parameters, or control retuning: e.g. for the shifted weight `|∂β log Z|≤P`, so replacing the doubled-box β alone costs up to `P(2n)|δβ|`, not a uniform small error merely from leading-log agreement.
* **Coarse reflection positivity:** required if applying the recurrence **to the effective actions themselves** (and then one needs common rectangular time-independent trace kernels, not merely RP on periodic cubes). Not required to use RG solely to bound the *fine* Wilson seed, since fine (H) is available independently. No proof of coarse (H) found in the cited result.
* **Free energy:** distinguish accumulating an RG approximation error from estimating an exact difference. The normalized block step preserves total **unnormalized** mass exactly on its own trajectory (informalization, part1/05 lines 251–254; program overview line 85). The normalized Wilson probability in `PublicWilson.lean:29–33` alone loses the scalar. Keep the scalar normalizations, match β, bound `16 log Z_n−log Z_{2n}` including nonextensive terms with sufficient margin below threshold. Neither two extensive error bars nor small polymer activities without a finite-volume remainder do this. These are relevant audit questions, not evidence for a seed at the weak coarse coupling. I did not audit the full RG development or prove equality/inequality of any two chosen bare couplings.

## 5. N05: possible torelons, but no measured seed scale

**Mechanism — plausible, not measured (N:32–43).** On a periodic spatial box a centre-neutral pair of oppositely wound flux tubes can mix with the scalar singlet; a single fundamental winding loop is centre charged. Teper Table 10 gives `a√σ=.1834(26)` at 2.5 on 16⁴ and `.1881(28)` on 20⁴. With `s=a√σ`, the first two long-string terms give `2aE≈2(s²L−π/(3L))`. Using .1834 gives .276,.463,.633,.792 at L=8,10,12,14, reproducing N:34; using the printed rounded .18 literally gives .257,.439,.603,.758. This is a truncated long-string formula for noninteracting strings, not the exact Nambu–Goto energy. Short strings, interactions, finite T and overlap with other excited states matter. To test mixing, include centre-neutral **pairs of spatial** winding Polyakov loops projected to the scalar channel alongside the plaquettes; measure single-torelon energies independently and check the mixed spectrum versus L. A temporal Polyakov loop or just a better one-state fit is not that test.

**Reject N:40–43 as an inference.** Failure of two one-state fits at L=8–12 neither identifies the lowest level nor proves a crossing at `L√σ≈2.6`; fit success from L=14 does not identify an infinite-volume eigenstate either. N:41 also mixes a t=2 effective mass with a window fit: at L=10 the t=2 log/cosh masses are .633/.635, not the [2,5] fit .624. A dip in any of these estimators is not nonmonotonicity of the full finite-box transfer gap. A single β, no string-width measurement and one observable channel do not test a uniform physical size in flux-tube widths.

There **is** a useful necessary condition, but it shows exactly what is missing. By the first-edge bound in §1, a seed at side n requires `exp(−n m_box) ≤ exp(1/(8·7480))−1`, i.e. `n m_box ≥ 10.9994` for the true first excitation at that side (infinite for rank one). A certified *upper* bound on such an excitation below `10.9994/n` could **exclude** that n. A noisy finite-T effective mass is not that certificate, and passing this necessary condition cannot bound the excited-state multiplicities or the partition-function defect from above. No absolute Δ was measured. Keep the matched-volume Teper check; remove “lower bound on the scale” and any assertion of a measured threshold for the seed.
