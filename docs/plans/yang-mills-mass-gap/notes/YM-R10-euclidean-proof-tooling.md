## Result and truth labels

- **Established framework, cited:** Euclidean correlations satisfying the full applicable Osterwalder–Schrader hypotheses reconstruct a positive-energy relativistic theory. Reflection positivity, growth/regularity, covariance, symmetry and the vacuum/clustering conditions must be accounted for. Use OS1975 as well as OS1973: the former corrects and extends the latter.
- **Standard spectral calculation, displayed in the notes:** a common positive Euclidean decay rate on a family of vacuum-subtracted states spanning the physical vacuum sector excludes all energies below that rate. One correlator tests only the spectral states it sees. The existing manuscript Proposition `ym:prop:gap-decay` supplies the general dense-set formulation.
- **Research obligation, open here:** construct the appropriate continuum/infinite-volume gauge-invariant correlations and preserve the required axioms, nontriviality and estimates through those limits. A finite-volume gap or a numerical fit is not that result.
- **Source statement, quoted verbatim in the notes:** Jaffe–Witten p6 requires a nontrivial theory on R4 for every compact simple gauge group, with a positive gap. The literal definition also requires the supremal gap mass m to be finite. Delta is an admissible bound; m is its supremum, not necessarily an eigenvalue.
- **Clustering exposition:** the notes expand vacuum subtraction and large-distance factorization, distinguish spacelike commutativity from vanishing connected correlations, and distinguish Euclidean-time spectral decay from the relativistic spatial clustering theorem. An explicit regulated free massless scalar integral gives 1/(4 pi^2 r^2), illustrating clustering without a gap. The manifold extension quoted by Jaffe–Witten remains heuristic here.

## Reusable Rust and symbolic work

Coordinate with YM-T15 (signed group words/trace), YM-T09 (exact Haar/matrix integration), YM-N01 (sampling) and YM-G02 (the proposed IR argument). Do not expand or close those packets from this reading task.

1. **Represent the mathematical assumptions.** Separate Euclidean time reflection, oriented link/path reflection, conjugation, group/representation, normalized measure, observable support and physical units. Time-link reflection may reverse orientation; derive the full endpoint map before coding. Gauge-fixed propagator positivity is not a substitute for positivity of the physical gauge-invariant observable sector.
2. **Build finite reflection Gram examples.** For positive-time observables F_i, form M_ij=E[overline(F_i(theta U)) F_j(U)]. Reuse exact word/trace and Haar primitives rather than a chapter-specific solver. Check Hermiticity and, where justified, exact factorization M=B*B or rational PSD criteria. A finite set of passing matrices is a check, not a proof of reflection positivity for all observables. Negative or uncertain numerical eigenvalues require precision/error analysis before being called counterexamples.
3. **Use independent controls.** A free massive scalar mode has kernel exp[-omega(s+t)]/(2 omega), omega>0, on positive times: its Gram matrix is rank-one positive. On lattice times, represent alpha=exp[-a omega] as a positive parameter with declared domain, so the bounded symbolic example does not require a general exponential integrator. Include an intentionally broken reflection/kernel as a negative control, and explicit scalar or direct-matrix calculations independent of the symbolic simplifier.
4. **Preserve spectral coverage and units.** Record which observable-generated states are controlled, vacuum subtraction, renormalization, volume, cutoff and coupling trajectory. Relate a transfer eigenvalue ratio r to physical energy -log(r)/a; a fixed measured gap at one spacing/volume does not supply a uniform lower bound along the chosen limits.
5. **Numerical work after exact semantics.** Estimate connected gauge-invariant correlations in Euclidean time with uncertainty/autocorrelation checks; compare solvable examples and multiple observable channels before interpreting an exponential fit. CUDA C++ comes after a useful CPU workload and profile, with identical CPU/GPU samples and end-to-end timing. Neither sampling nor acceleration proves the limit or the axioms.
