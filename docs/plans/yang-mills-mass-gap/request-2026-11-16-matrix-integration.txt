Your pasted passage interleaves the screen PDF’s columns. Let’s begin with the finite-lattice measure; the beta-function calculation can follow.

A configuration assigns a group matrix to every link. Here ℓ counts sites per direction, while a is the physical spacing:

Λ_{ℓ}=(ℤ/ℓℤ)⁴,  x=ay,  L=aℓ.

For G⊂ U(m), each positive link carries U(y,μ)∈ G. Thus a configuration consists of 4ℓ⁴ group matrices. The reverse direction uses the inverse of an existing matrix:

U(y,-μ)=U(y-e_{μ},μ)⁻¹.

The shifted origin matters.

A plaquette follows one elementary square. Its four matrices multiply in path order:

Uₚ=U₁U₂U₃⁻¹U₄⁻¹.

The inverses correspond to traversing the last two links backwards. For a nonabelian group, this order matters. Under a gauge transformation, the intermediate endpoint factors cancel, leaving

Uₚ^{γ}=γ(y)Uₚγ(y)⁻¹.

Consequently its trace is gauge invariant.

The Wilson measure tells us how to average configurations. Write

                1
S_{W}(U)=β∑ (1- ─RetrUₚ).
          p     m

Then

      e^{-S_{W}(U)}
dμ(U)=───────────── ∏ₑ h(dUₑ),   Z=∫ e^{-S_{W}(U)}∏ₑ h(dUₑ).
            Z

Here h is normalized Haar measure—the invariant probability measure on the compact group. At fixed lattice size, this is a finite-dimensional group integral. We integrate over matrices constrained to lie in G.

At β=0, the links are independently Haar distributed and Z=1. Positive β favors plaquettes closer to the identity and generally couples neighboring links.

An observable is a function of the configuration:

⟨𝒪⟩=∫𝒪(U) dμ(U).

A Wilson loop is one such observable: take the normalized trace of the ordered product around a closed path.

For defining SU(m), with tr(TᵃTᵇ)=δᵃᵇ/2, the coupling dictionary is

┌───────────┐
│   2m   1  │
│ β=──= ─── │.
│   g²  g₀² │
└───────────┘

Thus the conventional coefficients are 4/g² for SU(2) and 6/g² for SU(3).
